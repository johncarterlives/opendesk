Every conversation in opendesk is a **ticket**. Email, live chat, the API and the help center portal all create tickets in the same inbox, so your team works one queue. The inbox updates live: when a teammate replies, changes a status or assigns a ticket, everyone sees it without refreshing.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/overview" alt="The inbox: folders and views in the sidebar, the ticket list, and an open email ticket with its timeline, reply box and details panel, each part numbered" />

<Legend>

1. **Folders**: Unassigned, My tickets, All open, Pending, Solved and Spam, each with a live count.
2. **Views**: saved filters your team shares, like Urgent or Billing queue. See [Saved replies and views](/guides/saved-replies-and-views/).
3. **Ticket list**: the tickets in the folder or view, with search, filter and sort at the top.
4. **Ticket actions**: **Solve**, **Snooze**, and the **⋯** menu to assign to yourself, merge, copy a link, print or save as PDF, mark as spam or delete.
5. **Timeline**: the customer's messages, your replies, internal notes and every change, in order.
6. **Reply box**: write to the customer, or switch to **Note** to write only for your team.
7. **Details**: status, priority, severity, assignee, team, brand, the address replies go out from, and labels.

</Legend>

## Answer a ticket

<Steps>
<Step title="Open a folder, then a ticket">

Pick a folder or a view in the sidebar, then click a ticket in the list. Use the arrows at the top of the ticket, or <kbd>j</kbd> and <kbd>k</kbd>, to move to the next one.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/open-ticket" alt="The inbox with the All open folder ringed as 1 and a ticket in the list ringed as 2" />

</Step>
<Step title="Write a reply or a note">

**Reply** writes to the customer. On email tickets it is sent from the ticket's mailbox with a signature (the team's, else the address's, else your own), so the customer sees your support address. **Note** writes an internal note that customers never see, over email or in the chat widget. Type <kbd>/</kbd> to insert a saved reply, or `{{` to insert a [variable](/guides/email-templates/variables/) such as the customer's first name.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/reply" alt="The reply box with a drafted reply: the Reply and Note tabs ringed as 1, the Send button and its menu ringed as 2" />

</Step>
<Step title="Send, and set the status in one go">

Click **Send**, or press <kbd>mod</kbd> <kbd>enter</kbd>. The arrow next to it sends and sets the status in the same step: **Send and mark pending**, **Send and mark on hold**, or **Send and mark solved** (<kbd>mod</kbd> <kbd>shift</kbd> <kbd>enter</kbd>). An admin can put **Send and solve** on a button of its own next to **Send**, in **Settings**, under **Workspace**, **Statuses and priorities**; with **solved** labelled **Closed** it is **Send and close** (or **Send and mark as** your finish label). On a narrow reply box the formatting buttons fold into one to keep it in view. Each reply then shows its delivery status: pending, sent, failed or bounced.

Once a send finishes the ticket, or sets a status that takes it out of the list you opened it from, the next ticket of that list opens, as the next button would, with the list's filters and sort; after the last one the list shows again. Undoing the reply brings you back to the ticket, with the reply in the reply box. To stay on the ticket instead, turn off **Open the next ticket after "Send and solve"** in **Settings → Profile → Appearance**. It applies to the browser you set it in.

</Step>
<Step title="Check who else is on the ticket">

On an open ticket you see who else is viewing or replying, and on chat tickets the customer's typing. If a teammate is already replying, the bar above the reply box says so, and **Leave a note instead** switches you to a note.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/presence" alt="The bar above the reply box ringed: Priya is replying to this ticket right now, with Leave a note instead" />

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout>

- <kbd>mod</kbd> is <kbd>⌘</kbd> on macOS and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> elsewhere. Press <kbd>?</kbd> anywhere for every shortcut, or see [Keyboard shortcuts](/guides/keyboard-shortcuts/).
- Attachments upload once and are linked to the message when it is sent. Click a file's name to open it in a new tab and check it before sending.
- In the reply box, <kbd>mod</kbd> click a link, or use the button beside its address in the link box, to open it in a new tab.
- A new reply from the customer reopens a solved ticket.

</Callout>

## Set status, priority and assignment

Everything about a ticket is in the details panel on the right. Click a value to change it; the change shows up for the whole team at once and is recorded in the timeline.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/details" alt="The details panel: status, priority and severity ringed as 1, assignee and team ringed as 2, labels ringed as 3" />

<Legend>

1. **Status**, **priority** (low, normal, high or urgent) and an optional **severity**, from Sev 1 (critical) to Sev 4 (low impact), shown as a compact chip such as `S2`.
2. **Assignee** and **team**. Assign a person, a team, or both. Assigning someone notifies them (see [Notifications](/guides/notifications/)).
3. **Labels**, to group and find tickets. See [Labels and custom fields](/guides/labels-and-custom-fields/).

</Legend>

To keep these in view and the customer at the top of the details panel, set **Ticket properties** to **In a bar above the conversation** in **Settings → Profile → Appearance**. Status, priority, severity, assignee, team, brand, reply address and labels then sit in a bar under the ticket's header, each opening the same picker, with the same shortcuts. The bar wraps onto a second line when the pane is narrow, and the header no longer repeats status, priority, severity or team. The details panel starts with the contact and the [ticket panels](/guides/integrations/#ticket-panels), followed by custom fields, Ollie, links, side conversations and the rest. Custom fields stay in the panel, where each has room for its own editor. It applies to the browser you set it in.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `open` | Waiting on your team. |
| `pending` | Waiting on the customer. |
| `on_hold` | Waiting on something else, such as another team or a third party. |
| `solved` | Resolved. A new reply from the customer reopens the conversation. |
| `closed` | Finished and kept for the record. The customer cannot reopen it: an email that answers it opens a new ticket, a help center reply leaves it closed, and the chat widget asks them to start a new conversation. |

An admin can rename, recolour or hide statuses in **Settings**, under **Workspace**, **Statuses and priorities**. A hidden status leaves the menus agents pick from; a ticket already in it keeps it, and **Snooze** still works when **on hold** is hidden. **Open** and **Solved** always stay available. To call the finish action **Close**, rename **Solved** to **Closed** and hide the terminal **Closed** status. Finishing still sends satisfaction surveys when enabled, and a customer reply reopens the same ticket.

If your team ranks tickets by priority alone, an admin can turn **Severity** off in the same place. Severity then leaves the ticket's details, the list, filters and sort, views, bulk changes, automation conditions and actions, triage and the reports, and Ollie no longer sets or suggests it. Tickets keep the severity they had, the API still reads and writes it, and automations that use it keep running. Turn it back on to see it again.

A workspace can also assign new tickets automatically, by round robin or by load, in **Settings**, under **Assignment**, and [automations](/guides/automations/) can assign by rule. See [Triage and assignment](/guides/triage-and-assignment/).

## Create a ticket for a customer

Click **New ticket** and pick how it starts:

- **Email the customer** sends your first message from one of your addresses.
- **Start a chat** opens a chat in the customer's widget (see [Live chat](/guides/live-chat/)).
- **Log request** records a request that came in somewhere else, such as a phone call or a conversation. The ticket starts with what you write as the customer's own message, as if they had emailed it, and nothing is emailed. It waits for an answer like any new request, its response targets start as usual, and your replies go to the customer by email from the address you picked.
- **Log internally** starts the ticket with a note only your team sees.

Use **Log request** when the customer asked and the ticket should read that way; use **Log internally** for something you noticed yourself.

## Snooze, merge, spam and delete

**Snooze** hides a ticket until a time you pick: in an hour, in four hours, tomorrow, next week, or a date and time of your own. A snoozed ticket is on hold until then.

An admin can change the times the menu offers in **Settings → Workspace**, under **Snooze options**: up to eight, each in a number of hours, in a number of days at a time of day, or next week (Monday) at a time of day, such as tomorrow, in 3 days and in 7 days at 09:00. Each agent's menu works them out on their own clock, and a date and time of your own stays available. **Restore the defaults** brings back the four above.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/snooze" alt="The Snooze button ringed as 1 and its menu ringed as 2: In 1 hour, In 4 hours, Tomorrow, Next week, or a date" />

The **⋯** menu holds the rest: **Merge into another ticket**, **Copy link**, **Hide ticket history** (or **Show**), **Print or save as PDF**, **Mark as spam** and **Delete ticket**.

**Merge into another ticket** first offers the customer's other tickets, closed ones included, and leaves out those already merged elsewhere; type to search every ticket. Merging into a solved or closed ticket reopens it when the ticket you merge is still open, pending or on hold, so the customer's new message is not left in a finished conversation. Apart from changing its status, this is the only way a closed ticket opens again. If the ticket you pick was itself merged, the merge goes into the ticket it went into, and that is the ticket that opens.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/more-actions" alt="The More actions button ringed as 1 and its menu ringed as 2: Merge into another ticket, Copy link, Mark as spam and Delete ticket" />

**Delete ticket** (here or from the bulk bar) takes the ticket out of every list, view and search. It is not gone: an admin finds it in **Settings → Trash**, with who deleted it and when, and **Restore** brings it back as it was, conversation included. A ticket that was merged into another stays there; its conversation is on the ticket it went into.

## Print or save as PDF

You can print a ticket's conversation or a single message, or keep it as a PDF file. Any member can do this, viewers included.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the menu">

For the whole conversation, open the **⋯** menu at the top of the ticket and choose **Print or save as PDF**. For one message, open the **⋯** menu on that message instead.

</Step>
<Step title="Choose what to include">

For the conversation, choose what to include. **Include internal notes** and **Include side conversations** are off by default, so the printout holds only what the customer and your team said to each other. Click **Open print view**.

</Step>
<Step title="Print or save the file">

A print view opens and the print dialog follows once it has loaded. To keep a file, choose **Save as PDF** as the printer. The file is named after the ticket, like *#1234 Refund for order*.

</Step>
</Steps>

The print view is a plain, light page whatever your theme: the ticket's number, subject, requester and status, then each message with its author, date, recipients, text and the names of its attachments. Pictures pasted into a message print with it. A deleted message prints as the marker the timeline shows. **Print** in the bar at the top prints again, and **Close** (or <kbd>Esc</kbd>) takes you back to the ticket.

Remote images in a customer's email stay hidden in the print view too, unless you loaded them in the ticket or turned on **Always show images**. To print them, turn on **Show remote images** in the bar at the top. The sender's server can then tell the email was opened.

In the [desktop app](/guides/desktop-app/) the system's print dialog opens. To keep a PDF there, use **PDF → Save as PDF** on macOS, the **Microsoft Print to PDF** printer on Windows, or **Print to File** on Linux.

## Work on many tickets at once

Hover a ticket and tick its box (or press <kbd>x</kbd>), then tick any others. The bar at the bottom of the list changes them all in one step: assignee, team, status, priority, severity, labels, spam, or delete.

## Find tickets

The **filter** button at the top of the list narrows it by priority, severity, channel, label, team, brand, address or custom field. Save a filter you use often as a [view](/guides/saved-replies-and-views/), and it appears in the sidebar with its own count.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/filter" alt="The filter button ringed at the top of the ticket list, with its menu open: Priority, Severity, Channel, Label, Team, Brand, Address and Fields" />

The **sort** button beside it orders the list by last activity, creation date, priority, severity, SLA due or a custom field, newest or oldest first. Each folder, view and team inbox keeps the order you chose for it: open it again from the sidebar or a shortcut and it comes back the same way, until you choose **Last activity**, **Newest first** again. It applies to the browser you set it in; a link that names a sort opens in that one.

Press <kbd>mod</kbd> <kbd>k</kbd>, or click **Search** at the top, to search everything at once: tickets, contacts, articles and settings.

<Screenshot src="/guides/inbox/search" alt="The command menu searching for fingerprint, with a matching ticket listed" />

## What a ticket holds

- a **number** (`#1042`), unique and increasing within the workspace
- a **subject** and a **channel**: `email`, `chat`, `api` or `portal`
- a **status**, a **priority** and an optional **severity**
- an optional **assignee**, **team**, **mailbox** and **labels**
- the **contact** who wrote in, and their company when known
- an **SLA** with first response and resolution due times, when a policy applies (see [SLA policies](/guides/sla-policies/))
- a **CSAT** score once the customer rates the conversation (see [CSAT](/guides/csat/))

</Fold>

## Replies, notes and activity

A ticket's timeline holds its messages and its activity:

- **Replies** go to the customer. Each one shows its delivery status: pending, sent, failed or bounced.
- **Notes** are internal. Customers never see them, over email or in the chat widget.
- **Side conversations** are email conversations with someone outside your team, such as a supplier or a payment provider. See [Ask someone outside your team](#ask-someone-outside-your-team).
- **Activity** records status, priority, severity, assignment, team, label, SLA, merge and CSAT changes, with who made them.

An email reply goes to the customer's address, or to the `Reply-To` their latest email asked for when that address is at their own organisation (`alerts@updates.example.com` asking for answers at `ana@example.com`). To send it somewhere else, click **To** above the reply box and change the addresses; the customer's address and the `Reply-To` they named are one click away. The ticket's contact stays the same.

On a busy ticket, **⋯ → Hide ticket history** keeps the messages and calls only; **Show ticket history** brings the activity back. It applies to the browser you set it in, and the history shows by default.

To read a ticket like a chat, turn on **Conversation layout** in **Settings → Profile → Appearance**: the customer's messages sit on the left, and your team's replies and internal notes, Ollie's and automations' included, on the right. Activity stays full width, and on a narrow screen every message does. It applies to the browser you set it in.

On a wide screen the conversation and the reply box keep to a width that reads easily, centred in the ticket. To give them the whole width, set **Conversation width** to **Full width** in the same place; in the conversation layout the messages then grow with it, each side keeping its offset. Designed emails keep their own width, and on a narrow screen nothing changes.

A web address written as plain text in a message or a note, like one an integration posts through the API, opens as a link in a new tab. Only how it shows changes: the message keeps its text as it was sent.

An email you receive keeps its remote images hidden until you click **Load images**, so opening it tells the sender nothing. To show them at once in every email, turn on **Always show images** in **Settings → Profile → Appearance**. The sender's server can then tell when the email was opened. It applies to the browser you set it in.

### Your unsent reply is saved

What you write in the reply box is saved as you go, on the server. Close the tab, refresh, or open the same ticket on another device, and your reply is back in the box with its files. It is yours alone: teammates don't see it, and it is cleared when you send the reply or empty the box. A draft you don't touch for 30 days is removed. A reply a teammate or an AI agent staged for the ticket waits as a separate banner while you have your own text in the box.

### Take back, turn into a note or delete

- **Undo send**: an email reply waits a few seconds before it goes out (5 by default); a chat reply goes at once unless you give chat a wait too. Both are set in **Settings → Workspace**. Until then the timeline and a notice offer **Undo**, which puts it back in the reply box, **Turn into internal note**, and **Send now**. A reply that mentions a teammate with `@` asks first whether it was meant as a note. A reply that still holds a note in square brackets that an AI draft left to fill in, like *[confirm the refund amount]*, asks first too: **Fill it in** selects it, **Send anyway** sends the reply as it is. Links, `[#123]`-style references, quoted text and code don't count.
- **Turn into a reply**: from a note's menu, your note goes into the reply box. It is sent at the end of the conversation like any reply, and where the note was, a marker says it was sent as a reply, with **Show reply** to jump to it. Admins can still read the note as it was.
- **Turn into internal note**: on chat tickets, a reply already sent can still leave the customer's chat and stay as a note, though they may have read it. Email is delivered at once, so it cannot be taken back, nor can a chat reply already emailed to the customer because they had left.
- **Delete**: a deleted message leaves a marker saying who deleted it. Admins can read it and open its files, restore it, or delete it permanently, which removes its text and files for good (the ticket's subject stays). By default agents delete their own internal notes, and only admins delete replies. **Settings → Workspace** sets it for agents: off, their own notes, or any agent's notes and replies. An admin can give one agent a different rule from their menu in **Settings → Members**.

### Reply from your own address

With Collab, you can answer an email ticket from one of your own connected mail accounts instead of the support address, so a customer who knows you gets mail from you. Nothing changes until you choose it.

1. Above the reply box, click the address next to **Replying as** and pick one of **Your addresses**. Your private accounts are listed, along with shared accounts you can send from.
2. **Answers go to** sets the Reply-To: the address itself (or its own reply-to), the ticket's address, or any address you type. **Settings → Workspace** sets which one is the default.
3. Send as usual.

It goes out as your own mail, with that address's From name and signature, no email layout and no ticket number in the subject. It also shows in the account's Sent folder. The ticket keeps its reply address, and the reply is marked **Own address** in the timeline. When the customer answers your address, the answer comes back to this ticket like any customer reply: it reopens the ticket, counts for SLA targets and notifies the assignee. Only answers to these replies reach the ticket, never any other mail in your account. Your next reply on the ticket starts from the same address. Automations, auto-replies, CSAT emails and Ollie always use the ticket's address.

Admins set the defaults in **Settings → Workspace**, and each mailbox can set its own under **Replies from members' own addresses** in its settings: whether it is allowed, where answers go by default, and which addresses (any, shared accounts only, or only addresses at the mailbox's own domain). The rules of the ticket's current address apply, and the reply box offers only the addresses they allow. If the account is disconnected, no longer shared with you, or the rules change before the reply goes out, it fails and shows why.

### Ask someone outside your team

Some tickets need an answer from someone else first: a supplier, a logistics partner, a payment provider. Start a **side conversation** from the ticket instead of writing from your own inbox:

1. In the reply box, switch to **Side conversation** (or run **Start a side conversation** from the command menu, or click **New** under **Side conversations** in the ticket details).
2. Enter who to write to and your message. The subject starts as the ticket's; change it if it should read differently for them. Saved replies work here too, without the customer's or the ticket's details; Ollie's drafting is off, since it writes from the conversation with the customer.
3. Only your message goes out unless you add more: open the menu next to **Send** and, under **Include**, tick **The conversation** to quote the last messages with the customer (up to 10) below yours, or any of the files they sent. What you add shows next to your attachments, where you can take it out again. Internal notes never go along.
4. Send. To wait for them, choose **Send and mark on hold** from the send menu.

It is emailed from the ticket's support address, with your signature and only what you chose to include. Their answers come back into the same ticket, in the timeline and under **Side conversations** in the ticket details, and the customer never sees any of it. When they answer:

- The ticket's assignee is notified.
- A ticket waiting on the customer or on hold opens again, so you can pick it up.
- Nothing counts as a customer reply: SLA targets, response times, reports and automations are not affected, and no auto-reply goes out.

To pass on one email as it is, choose **Forward** in the menu of that message (an email from the customer, or a reply your team sent). A side conversation starts with the email below what you write: a header with who sent it, when, its subject and who it was to, then the email with its formatting and its images where they were. Its files are already added next to your attachments; take out any that should not go. You can send it without writing anything. A forwarded email replaces **The conversation** for that message.

**Reply** from the latest message of a side conversation to write in it again. **Close** it when you have what you need; a new message opens it again.

## Contacts and companies

Every customer is a **contact**, matched by email address, or by your own user id when the chat widget is [verified](/guides/live-chat/identity-verification/). Contacts can belong to a **company**, carry notes and custom fields, and be blocked. A contact's page lists all of their tickets. Open **Contacts** in the sidebar to browse them.

Beside the search, the **People** and **Companies** lists have a **Label** filter, a **Field** filter for your custom fields and a sort menu. A field with options, people or yes and no filters on the values you tick; any other field on whether it **Has a value** or **Is empty**. People can also be filtered to one **Company** (or **No company**) and to those who are **Blocked** or **Not blocked**, and sorted by name, by when they were added or last updated, or by how many tickets they have; companies by name, age, last update or how many people they have. Each filter shows as a chip you can clear. The filters and sort are in the page's address, so you can share a filtered list.

Tick rows to act on many at once: add or remove a label, set or clear a custom field, set a company (People), or delete. **Select everyone who matches** extends the selection to every record the search and filters find, up to 1000. Deleting a person keeps their tickets, without a customer; deleting a company keeps its people, without a company. A deletion cannot be undone.

### Invite people to the customer portal

Customers can follow and answer their requests in the customer portal of a brand whose help center is on. Instead of waiting for them to find it, invite them: open the contact, and under **Customer portal** click **Invite to portal**. They get an email with a link to choose a password, which works for 7 days and once. When more than one brand has a help center, choose the brand first.

**Customer portal** shows where each person stands: **No access**, **Invited** with when the link stops working, or **Has access** with their last sign-in. **Send a new link** sends another invitation, and **Remove access** signs them out of the portal, removes their password and closes open links. They can still ask for a new link on the portal's sign-in page, so block the contact to keep them out.

To invite several people at once, tick them in **People** and choose **Invite to portal**. People without an email address, blocked people and people who already have access are skipped, and the message says how many. A brand whose customers sign in through your identity provider needs no invitation: they sign in there.

### Several email addresses

When someone writes from a work and a private address, keep them as one contact: open the contact, choose **Edit**, and add the other address under **Other email addresses**. Mail from it then goes to the same contact, and a search finds them by it. **Make primary** swaps an address with the main one, which replies and the customer portal use. An address belongs to one contact, so adding one that another contact has is refused. Blocking a contact blocks all their addresses.

### Merge duplicates

When the same person already exists as two contacts, an admin can merge them: open the contact to remove, choose **Edit**, then **Merge into…** and pick the one that stays. Its tickets, messages and email addresses move to the contact you picked and the first one is removed. The contact that stays keeps its own name, phone, company and main address, and takes the other's where it has none. A blocked and a not blocked contact can't be merged, nor two contacts with different external ids. A merge can't be undone.
